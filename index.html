01Introduction

A homelab, drawn as a map of the machines that run it

A homelab is a handful of computers at home doing jobs people usually rent from big companies: storing files, streaming movies, running a game server.

Each shape on this map is one machine or service. The lines show how they connect.

The next nine chapters walk through it, one part at a time, including three projects I built here.

01Introduction
Runs on7 machines · 1 gateway

A small self-hosted stack with one way in

A Windows workstation, an Ubuntu server, a Synology NAS and a set of Raspberry Pis sit behind one gateway. Public apps are published through Cloudflare, never through open ports.

Browser → Cloudflare edge → Access policy → tunnel → app
  • Ingress: Cloudflare Tunnel, with no inbound port forwards.
  • Auth: Cloudflare Access single sign-on in front of private apps.
  • This page: static HTML on GitHub Pages. The status checks run in your browser.

02Front door

One door with a bouncer

Before anyone reaches a service, they come through one front door. A bouncer at that door checks who you are.

If you're on the list, you get in. If you're not, you never even see what's inside.

02Front door
Runs onCloudflare

Outbound tunnels and an identity check at the edge

Cloudflare Tunnel connects outbound from inside the network, so nothing listens on the home IP. Cloudflare Access puts single sign-on in front of each private app.

  • Launcher: lists every app a signed-in user may open.
  • Domain: DNSSEC on, DMARC enforced.
02Front door

The tunnel was the moment this stopped feeling scary. Nothing is exposed at home, and I can still reach everything from anywhere.

03Network

Quiet traffic control for every device

Every device at home asks for directions before it goes anywhere online. A tiny computer answers those questions, and it refuses to give directions to ads and trackers.

03Network
Runs onPi-hole + Unbound

Filtered, recursive DNS for the whole network

The gateway handles routing and DHCP. DNS goes to Pi-hole, which forwards to Unbound as a local recursive resolver.

Device → Pi-hole → Unbound → root servers
  • Result: no third-party DNS provider, and ad and tracker domains are blocked for every device.
03Network

The day I turned on network-wide blocking, everything in the house got faster. Nobody noticed, which is exactly how infrastructure should work.

04Media

Movie night that never buffers

The NAS is a big box of hard drives. Jellyfin turns what's on it into your own streaming service.

Seerr is the suggestion box, where you ask for something new.

Ask Seerr for a show and a team of little helper apps finds it, saves it to the NAS, and it shows up in Jellyfin. The dashed lines show that trip.

04Media
Runs onSynology NAS · media server

Library storage, playback and requests

Media lives on the Synology NAS. Jellyfin serves libraries and playback; Seerr takes requests and shows their progress.

  • Request flow: Seerr checks Jellyfin's library, then hands requests to Sonarr (TV), Radarr (movies) or Lidarr (music).
  • Containers: Prowlarr manages indexers, SABnzbd downloads, Bazarr adds subtitles, Recyclarr syncs quality profiles, and Unmanic tidies files on the NAS.
  • Status: the NAS chip checks Jellyfin's health endpoint from your browser.
04Media

This is the part my friends actually use. They don't care how it works. They care that it works.

06LED dashboard

4,096 tiny lights that know what's happening

A board of tiny lights on the wall cycles through the time, the weather, the sunrise and my photos.

When something is actually happening, it interrupts itself: a storm warning, a plane flying overhead, the space station passing, a song playing, or a phone sharing its screen to it.

06LED dashboard
Runs onRaspberry Pi 4 · 64×64 HUB75

A priority ladder that decides what the wall shows

Two Python processes and no database: a render loop at about 2 fps that pipes frames to a small C++ driver built on the rpi-rgb-led-matrix library, and a Flask app for setting it up from a phone.

AirPlay → flight overhead → space station → music → locked slide → rotation
  • Flights: its own ADS-B receiver first, public ADS-B feeds as backup, routes checked against the plane's position.
  • Faces: 33 clock faces, including a rendered Earth with the day and night line and a hand-tuned pixel aquarium.
  • Weather: forecast, a radar loop, severe weather alerts, air quality and UV.
  • Music and AirPlay: Spotify, Last.fm and HomePods, and phone mirroring scaled down to 64×64.
  • Testing: five suites, 1,013 checks.
06LED dashboard

My favorite project: 144 commits in four months, most of them late at night. The hardest bug was electrical. The Pi talks at 3.3 volts and the panel expects 5, so the picture tore until I slowed the signal way down.

It's useless in the best way.

07LLM Scanner

Can an AI be talked into things?

AI chatbots can be tricked into saying or doing things they shouldn't.

LLM Scanner is an app I built that downloads AI models onto one computer, chats with them, then throws hundreds of automatic trick questions at them. At the end it writes a report of what got through.

07LLM Scanner
Runs onOne machine · Gradio

A local red-team workbench for language models

A self-contained Gradio app that finds and downloads models from Hugging Face and the Ollama library, chats with them, and scans chat models with NVIDIA's garak.

Download → chat → garak probes → analyst report
  • Scans: saved probe groups for the OWASP LLM Top 10, a baseline assessment and NIST AI 600-1 risk areas, with pause, resume and stuck-probe recovery.
  • Reports: executive summary, OWASP mapping, findings with evidence and fixes, plus the raw data as CSV.
  • Red team tools: launches promptfoo and PyRIT locally against the installed models.
  • Upkeep: verified one-click updates that roll back if anything fails.
07LLM Scanner

I wanted to assess AI models the way I'd assess anything else: run the tests, keep the evidence, write it up. It shipped fourteen releases on its first day.

Studying for Network+ and Security+ goes a lot faster when there's something real to poke at.

08Hardening

Locking every door without locking myself out

Every computer in the lab used to accept passwords for remote logins, and passwords can be guessed.

I wrote scripts that switch every machine to digital keys instead, check each one for weak spots, and test every web page the lab serves from the outside.

08Hardening
Runs fromWorkstation · PowerShell + Bash

Key-only SSH with an automatic rollback

Install key → prove login → harden → verify → cancel rollback
  • setup-ssh-keys: installs an ed25519 key on each host and proves key login before anything else changes.
  • harden-hosts: switches a host to key-only SSH with a three-minute rollback, cancelled only after a fresh login works.
  • host-audit: a read-only snapshot of updates, open ports, firewall, accounts, sudo, logins, kernel settings, cron and Docker.
  • probe-services: unauthenticated requests to every web service, checking status, auth, TLS and headers.
08Hardening

The rollback timer is the part I like most. If a change locks me out, the machine undoes it by itself three minutes later.
